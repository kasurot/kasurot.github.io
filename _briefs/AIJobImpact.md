The prevailing narrative in IT strategy is that AI will automate the drudgery, freeing humans to move "up the stack" to higher-value strategic work.

This assumes that "Strategy" remains a scarce resource. Market mechanics suggest a different, more uncomfortable reality.

When the cost of building and planning drops to near-zero, the barrier to entry for strategy collapses. We are entering an era of infinite synthesis where perfectly framed plans are abundant. In this environment, having an idea is no longer a differentiator; it is the baseline.

We are witnessing a structural repricing of professional value:

### 📉 The Foundation (The Apprenticeship Void)
We are automating the "learning reps": drafting code, summarizing logs, writing initial briefs. While this creates massive efficiency gains today, it creates a structural **"Apprenticeship Void."**

In every craft, skill development follows an apprenticeship model. Juniors do the boring work. They struggle through it. They build the intuition that makes them seniors. Automate the boring work and you don't eliminate the bottleneck. You move it downstream. The system now has a shortage of senior architects with no way to produce them.

The apprenticeship void compounds. Year 1, you lose a few junior reps to automation. Year 3, your mid-level engineers have gaps in their foundational intuition. Year 5, you have no senior architects. We are eating our own seed corn to feed quarterly efficiency metrics, and the harvest is coming.

### ⚖️ The Spike (The Warranty)
As generation becomes cheap, the market shifts value to the one thing AI cannot provide: **Indemnity.**

The premium isn't on writing the plan. It is on physically warranting the outcome. It is the difference between the architect who draws the blueprint and the engineer who stamps it. One is an idea; the other is a liability.

This is a commitment device. When you sign your name, you are saying: "I own this outcome. If it fails, it fails on me." AI can generate a thousand plans. It cannot sign any of them. The sunk cost of AI efficiency gains is the senior talent pipeline you didn't build. The warranty is the scarcest resource in an age of infinite generation.

### 📉 The Middle (The Depreciation Layer)
The "Translation Layer" (business analysts, project managers, and architects who primarily document rather than decide) is depreciating. AI can draft the system design, map dependencies, and translate business requirements into technical specs instantly.

This isn't a new observation. What is new is the speed. The depreciation is happening faster than the repricing. Organizations are still paying premium rates for work that is rapidly approaching zero marginal cost.

### The Opportunity
The market is re-pricing professional value in real time. The people who thrive will be the ones who stop selling execution and start selling outcomes they're willing to sign their name to.

If you're building a career, the question isn't "Will AI take my job?" The question is "Am I building the kind of judgment that AI can't warrant?" If the answer is yes, the repricing works in your favor. If the answer is no, the repricing works against you.

When the Code is cheap and the Strategy is free, the only scarcity left is the Signature.
