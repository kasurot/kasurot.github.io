## The Problem: Identity Sprawl is a Hidden Tax on Your P&L

In the race for digital transformation, most enterprises have accumulated a portfolio of identity and access management (IAM) tools. You might have:

*   **Okta** for workforce SSO.
*   **Microsoft Entra** for your Microsoft 365 ecosystem.
*   **Auth0** or a homegrown solution for customer identity (CIAM).
*   **SailPoint** or **CyberArk** for privileged access and governance.

Each tool was likely the "best-of-breed" solution for a specific problem at a specific time. However, this fragmented landscape creates a significant, often unmeasured, drag on capital efficiency. It's a form of technical debt that manifests as a direct tax on your operating margin.

## The Math: Quantifying the Carry Cost

The true cost of identity sprawl extends far beyond licensing fees. The "carry cost" includes:

1.  **Redundant Licensing:** Paying for overlapping features across multiple vendors.
2.  **Integration Tax:** The high cost of specialized engineering talent required to build and maintain brittle integrations between these disparate systems.
3.  **Operational Friction:** Increased time-to-market for new applications due to complex identity onboarding.
4.  **Security Gaps:** A fragmented control plane creates a larger attack surface, increasing the potential liability from a data breach.

A conservative estimate places this carry cost at **1.5x to 2.0x** the stated licensing fees. A $500k/year portfolio of tools could easily represent over $1M in total cost of ownership (TCO).

## The Solution: Identity as a Consolidated Asset

The modern approach is to treat identity not as a collection of tools, but as a single, unified control plane. The goal is **liquidation**: strategically decommissioning redundant systems and consolidating their function into a primary platform.

This is not just a cost-cutting exercise; it's a capital allocation strategy. By liquidating the high-carry-cost "identity debt," you free up capital and engineering resources that can be reinvested into revenue-generating initiatives.
